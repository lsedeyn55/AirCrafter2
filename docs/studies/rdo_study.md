# Robust design optimisation: AirCrafter 2.0 study

Generated by `studies/rdo_study.py report` from `outputs/rdo/*.json` (the raw results, not in git; a compact copy is in `docs/studies/rdo_study_results.json`). Plan, screening and node-convergence check: `docs/development/RDO_PLAN.md`. Formulation: `docs/modules/robustness.md`.

**Read this first.** Every spread is a PLACEHOLDER (`configs/uncertainty.yaml`, PLACEHOLDERS Q32), as are the motor, battery and payload values. The objective is the placeholder 3-lap time with the 20 % energy reserve unless stated. The results show how the model behaves under RDO and what RDO costs, not a design.

## Summary

- **Single 10x7E 4S:** the robust design (μ + σ, β = 2) cuts P(energy margin violated) from 52 % to 1.6 % and P(SM violated) from 50 % to 3.7 %, and σ of the 3-lap time by 14 %, for +0.01 s of μ and +0.09 s nominal (§2).
- **Normality assumption:** P(violation) is at or below Φ(−2) = 2.3 % for the energy margin, slightly above it for SM (mostly the screening error, §2). A constraint outside the robust list (rpm_margin on the 10x7E twin) gets worse under RDO (§5, Q33).
- **Energy reserve:** β σ alone gives most of the protection of the 20 % reserve with less battery (§3).
- **Pareto front:** short and flat; most of the σ reduction comes from the robust constraints, not k (§4).
- **Enumeration:** the ranking twin 10x7E < single 10x7E < twin 8x6E holds under RDO (§5).
- **Propagation:** UDR (N = 15) is exact enough for the constraints and about 10 % low on σ of the lap time; Smolyak level 2 fixes that at 8.5 times the nodes (§1).
- **Cost:** a robust optimisation costs 17-25 times a deterministic one, about 16-19 times per iteration (N = 15 nodes, serial) (§6).

**Reading it.** The means agree within the Monte Carlo standard error for every output and every rule. For the constraints UDR's σ is within 0.5 % of the d = 7 Monte Carlo: they are close to additive in the parameters. For the 3-lap time UDR's σ is -9.5 % off while Smolyak level 2 is +1.2 %, so the lap time has interaction (cross) terms between the screened parameters that the univariate decomposition cannot represent (the powertrain parameters act together: Kv, Rm and the thrust factor all set the thrust and power curves). The screening adds 2-7 % (the 8 parameters held at their means), so against the full Monte Carlo UDR is low on σ by about 15 % for the lap time and 2-6 % for the constraints. In the robust objective μ + kσ (k = 1) that is about 0.1 s of a 80 s lap; in the constraints, β σ is about 5 % short. Smolyak level 2 removes the propagation error at 8.5 times the nodes; it is one config line (`robust.quadrature: smolyak`) if that matters.

P(violation) at the deterministic optimum, Monte Carlo over all 15: energy_margin 52.2 %, rpm_margin 0.4 %, SM 49.7 %; every other constraint 0 %. The two active constraints fail about half the time, as an optimum on its constraints must.

**What changed.** The robust design trades 23 g and +0.09 s of nominal lap time for a 14 % smaller σ (MC, 0.923 → 0.798 s); μ moves by +0.010 s, so μ + 1σ improves by 0.115 s. Most of the change is in the constraints, not the objective: capacity +15 % so that μ − 2σ of the energy margin is zero, and the battery and cargo move to their aft bounds (x_batt, x_cb_frac) to pull the mean static margin down to 0.160, so that μ + 2σ ≤ 0.2. The span grows (1.275 → 1.361 m) with the mass.

**P(violation) against the normality assumption.** β = 2 would give Φ(−2) = 2.3 % for a normal g. The energy margin comes out at 1.6 %. The static margin comes out at 3.7 %, above it. The quadrature is not the cause: UDR's σ of SM (0.0197) matches the Monte Carlo over the same 7 parameters (0.0197). The 8 screened-out parameters add 6 % to σ (0.0209), which puts the upper bound 1.89 σ from the mean instead of 2 σ (normal: 2.9 %); the rest is the shape of the distribution. Both are known, quantified costs of the choices made (screening, β σ with a normal reading).

## 3. Energy margin: does βσ replace the hand-picked 20 % reserve?

The energy constraint is (1 − reserve) E_usable − E_3laps ≥ 0 (deterministic) or its μ − 2σ form (robust). Probabilities from the Monte Carlo over all 15 parameters, on what the battery physically delivers: '3 laps' = E_3laps ≤ E_usable, '20 % left' = E_3laps ≤ 0.8 E_usable.

| Design | capacity (Ah) | E_usable, nominal (Wh) | E_3laps μ ± σ (Wh) | μ of usable energy left | P(not 3 laps) | P(less than 20 % left) | 3-lap time μ ± σ (s) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| det., reserve 0 | 0.708 | 8.38 | 8.43 ± 0.56 | -0.6 % | 52.2 % | 100.0 % | 80.09 ± 0.92 |
| det., reserve 0.2 | 0.886 | 10.49 | 8.45 ± 0.56 | 19.5 % | 0.0 % | 52.2 % | 80.12 ± 0.92 |
| robust, reserve 0 | 0.812 | 9.61 | 8.48 ± 0.57 | 11.7 % | 1.6 % | 91.8 % | 80.11 ± 0.80 |
| robust, reserve 0.2 | 1.016 | 12.03 | 8.50 ± 0.57 | 29.4 % | 0.0 % | 1.6 % | 80.13 ± 0.80 |

**Reading.** With no reserve, the deterministic optimum finishes 3 laps only about half the time: it is designed for the nominal parameters. The 20 % reserve fixes that with a large margin (no Monte Carlo sample fails to finish). The robust design with no reserve gets close to the same protection from β σ alone, with 8 % less capacity than the deterministic design with the reserve, and the risk it accepts is stated (the P(not 3 laps) column) rather than implicit. Stacking both (robust with the 20 % reserve) is the most conservative design. Which one is right depends on what the reserve is for: if it only covers the uncertainty in the model, β σ replaces it; if it is a rule or a safety allowance for things the model does not include, it stays. The σ here comes from placeholder spreads (Q32), so the size of the saving is not a result.

**Reading.** The front is short and flat: from min μ to min σ, σ falls by 0.020 s (3 %) for 0.045 s of μ. Along it the capacity hardly moves (the robust energy constraint sets it); σ is bought with span (b 1.33 → 1.49 m). Every point is far from the deterministic optimum in σ: most of the σ reduction comes from the robust constraints (§2), not from the weight k, so the choice of k matters little here. The front is in UDR estimates, about 13 % low on σ against the Monte Carlo (§1-2); the shape, not the level, is the result.

Ranking, deterministic (nominal 3-lap time): 2 x 10x7E 4S < 1 x 10x7E 4S < 2 x 8x6E 4S (matched power)

Ranking, robust (μ + σ): 2 x 10x7E 4S < 1 x 10x7E 4S < 2 x 8x6E 4S (matched power)

**Reading.** The ranking does not change: robustness costs every case a little, and none enough to swap places. σ reduction (deterministic → robust, same estimator for both): 1 x 10x7E 4S 14 %, 2 x 10x7E 4S 26 %, 2 x 8x6E 4S (matched power) 11 %. The source of risk differs by case: at the deterministic optima the binding uncertain constraints for the 10x7E designs are the energy margin and the static margin (each violated about half the time), while the 8x6E twin has energy to spare and only the static margin is at risk.

Constraints violated more often than Φ(−2) = 2.3 % in the robust designs (Monte Carlo over all 15): 1 x 10x7E 4S: SM 3.7 % (deterministic 49.7 %); 2 x 10x7E 4S: rpm_margin 10.0 % (deterministic 6.2 %); 2 x 8x6E 4S (matched power): SM 2.3 % (deterministic 49.8 %). Constraints outside `robust.robust_constraints` (e.g. rpm_margin) are only enforced at the reference node, so nothing bounds their probability, and RDO can make them worse, as it does for rpm_margin on the 10x7E twin. Adding one to the list is a config change, not done here so that every case uses the same settings (PLACEHOLDERS Q33). On the 10x7E twin the robust μ + σ lead over the single motor is about 3 s, far more than a robust rpm_margin is likely to cost, but that is not verified.

**5a. Screening check on the twin designs.** First-order variance covered by the single design's 7 parameters, at each twin's deterministic optimum: twin_10x7E: ThreeLapTime 93 %, energy_margin 98 %, SM 81 %, thrust_margin_static 96 %, stall_margin 97 %; twin_8x6E: ThreeLapTime 96 %, energy_margin 100 %, SM 88 %, thrust_margin_static 99 %, stall_margin 99 %. The static margin is below the 90 % target on both twins (the screened-out parameters matter more for it there), so a set screened on each twin could differ; the Monte Carlo above, over all 15 parameters, includes what the screening leaves out.

## 6. Cost: deterministic vs robust

Wall time on this machine (16 cores, 14 GB), each optimisation one process with 3 BLAS threads (two or three robust runs and a Monte Carlo ran at once, so these are upper bounds), from the config start point, SLSQP. Robust: UDR, d = 7, N = 15 nodes, serial (no MPI).

| Case | det. time | det. SLSQP its | robust time | robust SLSQP its | time ratio | per-iteration ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 x 10x7E 4S | 85 s | 39 | 24.8 min | 41 | 17.4 | 16.6 |
| 2 x 10x7E 4S | 112 s | 60 | 47.0 min | 94 | 25.1 | 16.0 |
| 2 x 8x6E 4S (matched power) | 73 s | 40 | 27.6 min | 51 | 22.8 | 17.9 |
| 1 x 10x7E 4S, reserve 0 | 77 s | 40 | 25.0 min | 41 | 19.4 | 18.9 |

The same single 10x7E robust problem run through the CLI (`aircrafter2 run configs/full_mdo_rdo.yaml`, with less running alongside) took 19.4 min and reached the same optimum (μ + σ = 80.7646 s).

For scale: the 1024-sample Monte Carlo used to *check* one design took about 6 min on 5 worker processes (≈ 30 CPU-min), and a Monte-Carlo-in-the-loop optimisation at that sample size would be roughly 1024/15 ≈ 70 times the robust cost above.
