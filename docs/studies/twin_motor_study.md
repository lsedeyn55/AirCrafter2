# Single vs twin motors: AirCrafter 2.0 study (twin-motor brief, checkpoint 3)

Generated by `examples/twin_motor_study.py`. Raw results are in `docs/studies/twin_motor_study_results.json`.
Figures (PDF + PNG) are in `outputs/twin_study/`, which is regenerated by the script and is not in git.

**Read this first.** Every motor, battery, ESC and prop mass and every rudder value is a PLACEHOLDER (OPEN_QUESTIONS Q7, Q8, Q23, Q25, Q29). Both layouts use the **same placeholder unit**: Kv 900, 0.15 kg, 60 A, 4S. So at the same prop and cells, the twin has **twice the installed power**. The objective is the placeholder 3-lap time with a 20% energy reserve. The numbers show how the trade behaves in this model; they are not a design recommendation.

## Setup

- **Configs:**
  - `configs/step7_full.yaml`: n = 1, a nose tractor at x = −0.30 m.
  - `configs/step7_full_twin.yaml`: n = 2 at y_m = 0.2515 m (Woombah 9.9") and x = −5.5" (Woombah firewall), one pack per motor, Q_nac = 1.3 (Woombah), nacelle 0.15 × 0.05 m (placeholder), m_nacelle = 0 (placeholder, Q25), `nacelle_torsion` on, `motor_inertia_relief` off, body nacelle form factor.
  - The two configs are otherwise identical: same DVs, bounds, tails, payload and mass model.
- **Outer enumeration (D4, D8):** n ∈ {1, 2} × prop ∈ {10x7E, 11x7E, 12x8E} at 4S. Then:
  - a matched-power set of twins;
  - relief on and the Raymer nacelle form factor, at the best twins.
- **Feasible** means SLSQP converged *and* the fixed current and RPM post-checks pass.

## 9. What the model can and cannot say about this trade

- **Same unit, twice the power.** The best twin is -2.77 s against the best single, but it installs 2.00× the static thrust and 2.00× the static power. That is a property of the placeholder powertrain, not of the layout.
- **Matched power.** At about the single's static electrical power, the best twin (8x6E 4S, 23.9 N, 508 W) is +2.39 s against the single. Here the layout effects are visible:
  - nacelle drag: C_D S ≈ 11 cm² with Q = 1.3 and the body form factor;
  - the second motor, ESC and prop;
  - the CG shift from the motors at the wing;
  - smaller props, which run at a different advance ratio and efficiency;
  - two packs: at the optimum each pack is at 0.50 Ah (the capacity lower bound, a placeholder). So one pack per motor forces surplus energy, and its mass, that the 3-lap objective doesn't need;
  - The match is only as close as the discrete prop and cell choices allow: ±10% on static thrust or ±5% on static power.
- **Matching static thrust is not enough.** The 3S twins match the single's static thrust but are +15 to +29 s slower. With the same Kv, a lower pack voltage lowers the no-load rotor speed, so the thrust falls off sooner with speed and the full-throttle level speed drops. Lap time is set by thrust at speed, i.e. by power and pitch speed. The comparison that means something is therefore the matched static *power* one (4S).
- **Slipstream is not modelled (PLAN §6h).** It is not neutral between the layouts:
  - the twin washes about 2D of clean wing, the nose prop mostly the fuselage;
  - leaving it out under-predicts the twin's take-off and turn C_L, and slightly under-predicts its washed-strip profile drag in cruise (a few percent of q there);
  - it also omits the single's fuselage scrubbing drag.
  - At the best designs here, none of the take-off-thrust, stall or turn-speed constraints is active (§1, §2). The active ones are energy, stability, the tail volumes and DV bounds. So the omission is second order for these lap times; it would matter for a take-off-limited design.
- **Interference.** Q_nac = 1.3 is a single number from Woombah (after Raymer). Wing–nacelle interference depends on the nacelle's position and shape. The nacelle form factor is uncertain by 2–3×: Raymer's body form against the nacelle form, §7 and PLAN §6h T6. That uncertainty is worth about 0.4 s here, and the briefed body form is the pessimistic one for the twin. The nacelle mass is 0 (placeholder, Q25), which is optimistic for the twin: about 3.5 s per kg per nacelle.
- **Structure.** The twin carries the nacelle torsion load (conservative default). Bending relief is off by default. At the best twin the spar is at minimum gauge; at the matched twin it is load-sized, but relief saves under 1 g (§4). So the twin's structural benefit is negligible at this scale and load factor (n = 5). Thrust and motor-torque loads on the wing are not modelled.
- **Not modelled at all:**
  - the reasons Woombah chose a twin: clean flow at the nose for the glider and the airspeed sensor, and payload integration;
  - redundancy;
  - asymmetric thrust and handling, beyond the OEI post-check (§5);
  - counter-rotation;
  - ground clearance of the props on a taildragger.
  - Those are requirements and constraints, not lap-time effects. They belong in the scoring plug-in or the constraints of a specific year.
- **What the model can say:** at a given installed thrust and power, how much nacelle drag and motor mass at the wing cost in lap time, and how the optimum wing and tail respond. That is the sensitivity the AIAC paper can report. A verdict that one layout is faster in general would not be supported, because it rests on the placeholders above.
