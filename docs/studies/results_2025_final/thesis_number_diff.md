The code change moves F by at most 3.5e-08, so **every change on this page comes from the take-off**, plus the re-optimisation it causes and, for the multistart count only, the fallback. At fixed design the take-off costs 0.025 to 0.050 in F. Re-optimised, the best case loses 0.0256. Its active constraints are the same set as the published optimum's, but the published optima, unchanged, violate M2's energy and laps constraints with take-off on. This replaces §4's sentence on the take-off (about 0.02, from the uncalibrated model).

## Where the claims that no longer hold are, and what the data say

1. **Wind.** §4 Results ("any headwind costs it laps", robust-design paragraph), App. A ("Wind was what robustness bought"). A headwind now raises M2's laps at the mean wind, because the ground roll starts at an airspeed equal to the headwind and saves take-off energy (`mission/smooth_lap.py`, take-off roll). The misses follow build mass. Even in the published arrays build mass was the stronger driver of M2 laps (rank correlation −0.94 against −0.32 for wind), so the old wording was already generous.
2. **Multistart.** §4 Results ("In both years, six of the ten starts converged", "same score within about 1e-4", Reliability paragraph "four of the ten"), App. A ("As in 2025-26, six of ten", "all to one optimum", "the other four failed in analysis at the start design"). Now 8 of 10, to two airframes 0.0003 apart; the default start is in the lower group, so the best known deterministic F is 5.0740, not 5.0737. Attribution of 6 → 8: multistart 0 converges with the fallback off (take-off and start path), multistart 7 fails with it off at evaluation 5, as published (fallback).
3. **"4 failed at their first analysis"** (brief, App. A) was already wrong in the published data: evaluations 50, 0, 0 and 5, as §4 correctly says.
